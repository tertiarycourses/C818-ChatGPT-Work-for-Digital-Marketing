# Cook & Bake Academy brand voice

Use this skill for customer-facing writing and design. Keep the work recognisably Cook & Bake: hands-on learning from working chefs, practical skills, and food learners make themselves.

## Tagline

**Learn it. Make it. Take it further.**

Use the wording and punctuation exactly when a tagline is appropriate. Do not force it into every short message.

## Voice

Sound warm, practical, confident, and a little playful, like a working chef who enjoys teaching. Prefer short, clear sentences and concrete details.

**Do**

1. Say what learners will make or practise and what they can take home.
2. Make the teaching tangible: hands at work, room to ask questions, and guidance from working chefs.
3. Match the audience and format: welcoming to home cooks, useful to career changers, and easy for gift buyers to understand.

**Don't**

1. Use empty praise or guarantees in place of evidence.
2. Invent course details, chef names, fees, dates, locations, outcomes, or offers. Check the current source material for each claim.
3. Sound pushy, corporate, or overly cute; keep any playfulness light and related to cooking or baking.

**Banned wording:** “amazing”, “world-class”, “best”, “guaranteed”, “life-changing”, “unmissable”, “once-in-a-lifetime”, and unsupported superlatives such as “Singapore’s No. 1”. Replace them with a specific, verified benefit. Avoid discount language unless the offer is confirmed in current policy or campaign material.

## Visual identity

| Colour | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crust | `#B5652D` | Primary brand accent and calls to action |
| Ink | `#2B211B` | Main text |
| Cream | `#FBF7F1` | Main background and open space |
| Basil | `#3F6B4A` | Secondary accent, focus, or positive states |

The existing website also uses crust dark `#8F4B1F` for hover states. Keep the four brief colours as the core palette. Use readable contrast and leave breathing room around text.

Use bold Georgia for headings and a simple sans-serif for body copy, matching the existing website's typography.

## Photography and artwork

Show real food, ingredients, tools, and hands at work in natural light. Prefer believable preparation and finished dishes over staged poses. Let the food and making process carry the story, with enough uncluttered space for copy.

Avoid stock smiles, people posing at the camera, clip art, and visuals that promise a result the class does not deliver.

## Final check

Before handing back copy or design, check the exact tagline, banned wording, palette, and photo direction. Verify every factual course or offer claim against current source files; if a fact cannot be verified, omit it or mark it for confirmation.
